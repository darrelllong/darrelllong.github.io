# Blog citation review, 29 October 2026

Reviewed all 25 published posts in `posts/index.json`. The starting inventory contained 356 link occurrences: 263 distinct external URLs and 53 local URLs. Combining fragments left 260 external source documents. The review also followed 26 local publication links to their papers, book record, or solutions manual, and checked 14 unlinked bibliography entries in the Backblaze and performance posts.

The [final inventory](blog-citation-inventory.json) records each link and its location after the corrections. Drafts are outside this inventory.

## Method

Checked source identity and the passage, abstract, table, or record relevant to the surrounding claim. Checked quotations and page references, dates, bibliographic details, reported measurements, and mathematical statements. HTTP success alone did not establish relevance: some successful responses were maintenance pages or access challenges.

Used original papers, institutional archives, author copies, publisher records, and the cited repositories where available. For personal recollections, the author supplies the account; biographical links establish the identity and background of the people involved.

Local downloads and publication routes resolve. The Humanities CSV contains 264 records: 188 in the stated Senate cohort, comprising 115 current professors and teaching professors and 73 emeriti. The linked dataset was checked for the counts cited in the post. The CV, REINAS announcement, Senate document, and other local archival documents were inspected for their cited material.

## Bibliographies and access limits

The five unlinked Backblaze references were checked against publisher or bibliographic records and original abstracts: Proschan (1963), Vaupel–Manton–Stallard (1979), Holford (1980 and 1983), and Clayton–Schifflers (1987). Their titles, dates, volumes, and pages match.

The nine unlinked performance references were checked against original papers, publisher records, author bibliographies, or library catalogues: Student (1908), Fisher's fifth edition (1934), Wilcoxon (1945), Mann–Whitney (1947), Welch (1947), Ferrari (1978), Mytkowicz et al. (2009), Curtsinger–Berger (2013), and Intel's JCC white paper (2019). Intel's document explicitly describes the 32-byte boundary effect discussed in the post.

Some publisher sites blocked full-text retrieval. Kuroda and Presburger were verified through bibliographic records; Welch's publication details were verified on the publisher's page. Other affected theory references were checked through author copies or indexed original abstracts. This review does not claim to have read every referenced work in full.

The FAS JASON reference supplies background on the advisory group. Sally Ride's membership is author-supplied; NASA supports her flights, both accident investigations, and her death. The Lavoisier museum inscription is likewise the author's firsthand recollection. The linked Bellows translation numbers the quoted Hávamál passage as stanza 77, which the post correctly specifies.
